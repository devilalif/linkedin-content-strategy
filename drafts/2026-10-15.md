**Three direction check executions, each matched to the pre-check reading the Week 123 post identified:**

**Trajectory check.** The practitioner runs a two-week brief-first production rate measurement: what percentage of week-one and week-two's production documents opened from the resolution output the brief specified (brief-first execution) vs. from a topic outline that reconstructed the resolution output in the editing phase (topic-first reversion). If stable: Q2 weeks five through eight add one brief-first execution reinforcement to the production template (a starting-point check the writer runs before the first line of the draft, as Week 122 described for week two's production additions). If declining: the trajectory check becomes the recalibration check — two weeks of declining brief-first execution despite the week-two production additions is not a trajectory, it is a reversion pattern that the check was designed to interrupt.

**Recalibration check.** The practitioner identifies the specific brief specification element that the production execution reverted: the resolution output position (the writer's first document did not begin from the resolution output), the format grammar constraint testability (the constraint appeared in the editor's review notes rather than the writer's first-draft self-test), or the direction check calendar status (the check's date or deliverable was not confirmed in weeks one and two). One addition, not a brief redesign. Q2 weeks five through eight run the current brief specification with one production-side execution step added at the reversion point, and the recalibration check runs again in week six (two weeks after the addition is in production) to confirm the addition held.

**Scope check.** The practitioner runs the direction check with the adjusted deliverable definition the Week 123 pre-check reading produced — a narrower or broader scope than the preparation window's original definition, based on what two weeks of production data showed the check actually needs to read. The scope check's output is whatever the adjusted deliverable definition produces: a rate measurement, a brief specification review, an execution addition, a calendar confirmation. ### Idea 3 — Wellness Tip: The Second Block's Week-Two First Session Is the Arc's First Intra-Block Week-Over-Week Comparison

**Pillar:** Wellness & Discipline

**Angle:** Every Wellness Tip in the training frequency management arc has compared readings across sessions within a week, or across the second block against the first block. The second block's week-two first session is the arc's first intra-block week-over-week comparison: week two's three-marker readings against week one's three-marker pattern, within the same block, at the calibrated frequency parameter.

This is a different comparison from any the arc has produced before. Intra-block week-over-week comparison is not the same as comparing two blocks at the same block position. Week one and week two of the second block share the same loading architecture — same exercises, same volume structure, same frequency parameter (or the calibrated adjusted parameter the week-two decision produced). The only variable between week one and week two is the inter-session accumulation of week one's stimulus.

Three intra-block week-over-week readings the second block's week-two first session produces:

**RPE trajectory within the second block.** The week-two first session's RPE at the working weight against the week-one two-session average RPE at the same load. If RPE is declining week-over-week at the same load, the second block's stimulus is accumulating with the inter-session consolidation running ahead of the loading — the frequency parameter is within the recovery system's processing rate and the block is producing an adaptation signal. If RPE is holding flat week-over-week at the same load, the block is at the consolidation boundary — the stimulus and recovery are in equilibrium, and the adaptation signal is available but not yet producing a consistent RPE direction. If RPE is rising week-over-week at the same load without a volume increase, the inter-session consolidation rate is not keeping pace with the loading — the frequency calibration the week-two decision produced (hold, adjust, or extend) may need a further extension before week two's second session is committed.

**Resting HR trend within the second block.** The week-two morning resting HR against the week-one resting HR pattern at the same training-day positions. A week-two morning resting HR tracking below or at week one's equivalent-day resting HR confirms the second block's cumulative load is within the nervous system's recovery capacity at the calibrated frequency. A week-two morning resting HR trending above week one's equivalent-day pattern is the first intra-block consecutive-day resting HR elevation — not a cross-block comparison, but a within-block elevation signal that indicates the second block's cumulative load is building faster in week two than week one at the same volume.

**Opening-set force output direction.** The week-two first session's first two working sets at the target load against the week-one average opening-set force output. A week-two opening-set force output that holds or improves against week one's average confirms the inter-session consolidation is running at the calibrated frequency parameter — week two is not starting below week one's structural baseline. A declining week-two opening-set force output against week one's average is the intra-block version of the Week 123 Wellness Tip's inter-session insufficiency signal: the consolidation between week one's last session and week two's first session did not complete before week two's stimulus arrived.

**The intra-block week-over-week comparison's specific value.** The cross-block comparison (second block against first block) produces accuracy readings: was the frequency decision correct? The intra-block week-over-week comparison produces trajectory readings: is the current block building the adaptation signal correctly, at the calibrated frequency, before the second accumulation block's volume increment decisions arrive?

**Hook:** "Every frequency management comparison in this arc has looked across sessions within a week, or across two blocks at the same position. The second block's week-two first session is the first comparison that runs within a single block, week-over-week: week two's three-marker readings against week one's pattern, at the calibrated frequency. This is not a check on whether the frequency decision was correct. It is a read on whether the second block is building its adaptation signal at the trajectory the calibrated frequency was designed to produce."

**Why now:** Friday April 24, 2028. Two weeks into the second accumulation block (which began April 8), at the position where the second block's week-two first session has produced its three-marker readings at the calibrated frequency — the first intra-block week-over-week comparison the arc can produce.
