The sixth week of the accumulation block ended.

Not because the calendar said six weeks. Because the training log said the block had extracted what it could at the current volume.

The RPE at the target load — the same exercise, the same working weight, tracked across the accumulation block — had been declining week over week through weeks three and four. In weeks five and six, the decline flattened. Same load, same RPE, two consecutive weeks. No more adaptation signal clearing at the current volume.

The block-level marker said: the recovery system is processing this volume at its ceiling. The structural output rate has stopped improving.

Week seven is the deload.

---

Not the light week.

Not the rest week.

The deload is the block's structural consolidation output — the week when the six weeks of accumulated adaptation signal converts from stimulus load into structural tissue. The principle is not complicated: the adaptation signal from six weeks of accumulation does not consolidate during the accumulation block. The accumulation block's volume keeps the recovery system in the processing phase — handling the current week's load before the next session arrives. The consolidation phase requires a reduced load. That is the deload.

The competitor entering week seven understands this.

The competitor inside the first deload session experiences something the principle does not prepare you for.

---

The volume in week seven is sixty percent of week six's peak. Same exercises. Maintained intensity — the working weight is not reduced. Two to three working sets where the accumulation block ran five to six.

The training psychology that built across six weeks of accumulation has calibrated to five to six working sets. Two to three feels insufficient.

The impulse in the first deload session: add sets.

Not because the adaptation signal is there — the block-level markers said the adaptation rate was at the current volume's ceiling. Because the training psychology accumulated across six weeks expects more output from a session than two to three working sets delivers.

That impulse is the deload week's first signal.

Not a signal that the deload is wrong. A signal that six weeks of accumulation calibrated the training psychology upward — and that the reduced volume is asking the psychology to trust the consolidation principle rather than the session's volume output.

---

The first working sets of the deload session's target muscle group at the target load produce lower RPE than any session in the accumulation block's final two weeks.

Not because the weight is lower — the working weight is the same.

Because the recovery system, no longer under the accumulation block's weekly loading cycle, has begun the consolidation process the reduced volume permits. The RPE drop in the first deload session's first two working sets is the block's structural output beginning to consolidate — the confirmation that the reduced volume is not reducing development, but allowing the development the accumulation block produced to complete the process the accumulation block's volume was too high to permit.

This is the data point that resolves the impulse: the RPE at the same working weight has dropped, which means the consolidation is beginning. The deload's reduced volume is not interrupting the accumulation block's work. It is the accumulation block's work, converting from stimulus to structure.

Read the RPE drop. Trust the consolidation.
